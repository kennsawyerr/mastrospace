Stamped Concrete vs Interlock: Which Lasts Longer for a Driveway?

Mastrospace · 5 min read

Both can last for decades. The difference is in how they age, how they fail and how easy they are to repair. For most driveways in our climate, that difference matters more than the headline lifespan.

Stamped concrete

This is a poured concrete slab that is patterned and coloured while still wet, to look like stone, brick or slate. It gives a seamless, solid surface.

  • Strengths: a continuous surface with no joints for weeds to grow through, a wide range of patterns, and a clean modern look.
  • Weaknesses: concrete can crack as the ground moves or if the base is poor. Colour can fade under sun and wear, and it needs resealing every few years to stay protected.
  • Repairs: cracks can be filled, but matching the original colour and pattern is difficult, so repairs often remain visible.

Interlocking pavers

Individual concrete blocks laid on a compacted base and sand bed, locked together by their shape and the jointing sand.

  • Strengths: the surface flexes slightly with the ground, so it tolerates movement better. Colour runs through the block, and drainage through the joints reduces standing water.
  • Weaknesses: joints can collect weeds and moss, and pavers can sink or shift if the base was not properly compacted.
  • Repairs: a damaged or stained block can be lifted out and replaced, and a sunken area can be re-laid without touching the rest.

So which lasts longer?

In practice, the answer is whichever is built on the better base. A poorly prepared sub-base will ruin either one within a few seasons. Given equal preparation, interlock tends to age more gracefully on driveways because it moves with the ground and can be repaired piece by piece. Stamped concrete can look excellent for many years, but a single crack can affect the whole appearance.

What matters in Port Harcourt

  • Heavy rain: permeable joints on interlock help water drain, while a concrete slab needs correct slope and drainage channels.
  • Soft or sandy ground: needs a thicker, well-compacted base for both options.
  • Vehicle load: confirm the thickness and strength specified for the heaviest vehicles that will use the drive.
  • Maintenance: interlock needs occasional jointing sand top-ups and weed control; stamped concrete needs periodic sealing.

Questions to ask your contractor

  • How deep will the base be, and how will it be compacted?
  • How will rainwater leave the surface?
  • What is the thickness of the slab or blocks, and what is it rated for?
  • What maintenance should we expect, and what is the guarantee?
