What to Check Before Hiring a Landscaper in Port Harcourt

Mastrospace · 5 min read

Good landscaping changes how a property feels and what it is worth. Bad landscaping means dead plants, flooded gardens and paying twice. Before you sign anything, run through these checks.

1. Ask to see finished work, ideally in person

Photos are a start, but a landscaper who is confident will show you completed projects nearby, preferably ones that are at least a year old. Mature work tells you far more than a fresh install. Ask who the client was and whether you can speak to them.

2. Insist on a site visit before any quote

A price given over the phone is a guess. A proper landscaper walks the site and looks at the soil, the slope, where water collects, how much sun each area gets, and where access is tight. If nobody visits, expect surprises in the bill.

3. Look at how they handle drainage

This matters more in Port Harcourt than in most places. Heavy rain will test every garden. Ask how the design moves water away from the building, whether lawns and beds are graded properly, and what happens to runoff from paved areas. A beautiful design that floods is a failed design.

4. Check the plant choices

Plants should suit our heat, humidity and rainfall. Ask what they recommend and why, how big each plant gets, and how much care it needs. Be cautious if the proposal is full of delicate species that need constant attention.

5. Get a written, itemised quote

  • Materials and plants listed separately from labour
  • What is included, such as soil, mulch, clearing and waste removal
  • A start date, an end date and a payment schedule tied to progress
  • What is excluded, so extras are not a surprise

6. Ask who will actually do the work

Find out whether the company uses its own team or hires casual labour for each job, and who supervises on site. Consistency of crew often shows in the finish.

7. Understand the aftercare

New lawns and plants need watering and attention in the first weeks. Ask what care instructions you will get, whether there is a maintenance plan, and what happens if plants fail early. A short written guarantee on plants is a good sign.

8. Pay attention to how they communicate

Do they answer questions clearly, explain trade-offs and say no to ideas that will not work? That honesty during the quote stage usually carries into the project.
